# 03 Problem and public benefit

## The problem passengers face
1. **Journeys cross operators, but information does not.** A trip often has a BMTC bus leg, a Namma Metro leg and a
   walk between them. Passengers usually have to put these together themselves.
2. **Language.** Many passengers are more comfortable in Kannada, Telugu or Hindi than in English.
3. **A changing network.** New Metro sections and feeder bus routes open in stages. Press reports describe the Yellow
   Line opening in August 2025, and the elevated Pink Line and five BMTC feeder routes from 14 November 2026. Passengers
   need to know what is running today and what is only planned.
4. **Trust in information.** A passenger cannot easily tell an official timetable from an estimate or a copied list.
5. **Access.** Some passengers have low vision, use old phones, or travel with little mobile data or none.

This document makes no statistical claims about ridership or travel times. Measuring them is part of the pilot in
[13](13_PILOT_PROPOSAL.md).

## Other tools already exist
BMTC and BMRCL run their own passenger apps and websites. Private apps already plan bus and Metro journeys, and some
sell tickets through authorised channels. This application does **not** claim to be the first or the only one. Its
contribution is the combination below.

## Public benefit this application offers
| Benefit | How |
|---|---|
| One journey across bus, Metro and walking | Planner over both operators' timetables, with walking transfers and a bus/Metro/both choice |
| Four languages | The whole interface in English, Kannada, Telugu and Hindi; the choice is remembered on the device |
| Transparency about data | Every result names its source and is labelled official and verified, or unverified; a data-status screen lists each source's licence and permission |
| No false information | Live arrivals, tickets and payments are never imitated; the app says when information does not exist |
| Accessibility | Automated WCAG 2.1 AA checks pass; large text, dark mode and low-data mode; works on small screens |
| Low connectivity | Installable web app; screens and saved journeys open offline |
| Privacy | No accounts, names, phone numbers or locations stored; deletion on request |
| Openness | The source code and every test report are public, so any official can inspect them |
| Safety information | 112, 108, 101 and 1098 always one tap away; official helpline and website links |

## Who benefits
- Daily commuters who combine bus and Metro.
- Visitors and new residents who do not read English or do not know the network.
- People with low vision or other disabilities, and older passengers.
- Passengers with low-cost phones or limited data.
- Operators, through a channel that repeats their official information accurately and labels its source, at no cost to
  them.

## What it cannot do without government support
It cannot give accurate timetables, fares or live information, because these depend on official data that only the
operators can authorise. The benefits above become real for passengers only once licensed data is connected.
